What the columns mean

How teams are ranked. The AHL ranks teams by points percentage (PCT), not total points, because teams have played different numbers of games. Ties go to the team with more regulation wins (RW), then more regulation and overtime wins (ROW), then more total wins, then the better goal differential. Some sites, like FloHockey, sort by total points instead, so their order can differ from this one.

GP
Games played.
W
Wins of any kind: regulation, overtime, or shootout. Each is worth 2 points. Used to rank
L
Regulation losses, worth 0 points.
OTL
Overtime losses. Worth 1 point.
SOL
Shootout losses. Also worth 1 point.
PTS
Points: 2 per win, 1 per overtime or shootout loss.
PCT
Points percentage: points earned divided by points possible. A team with 6 points in 4 games has .750. Used to rank
RW
Regulation wins, not counting overtime or shootout wins. The first tiebreaker. Used to rank
ROW
Regulation and overtime wins, not counting shootout wins. The second tiebreaker. Used to rank
GD
Goal differential: goals scored minus goals allowed. A late tiebreaker, but mostly a sign of how well a team is really playing.
L10
Record over the last 10 games, as W-L-OTL-SOL.
STK
Current streak, like W3 for three wins in a row.
Playoffs
This site's estimated chance of making the playoffs, from simulating the rest of the season. Not an official AHL number.
▲ ▼
Places a team moved during last week's games (Monday to Sunday).

Records are written the AHL way, W-L-OTL-SOL, so 3-1-0-1 means 3 wins, 1 regulation loss, no overtime losses, and 1 shootout loss. On phones, swipe the table sideways to see every column; the team stays pinned on the left.
