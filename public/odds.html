What the columns mean

Everything on this page comes from this site's simulation of the rest of the season, not from the AHL. The odds explain how likely things are; only the standings decide who actually gets in.

Record
W-L-OTL-SOL: wins, regulation losses, overtime losses, shootout losses.
Games left
Regular-season games still to play.
Proj PTS
Projected final points, averaged across all the simulated seasons.
Proj PCT
Projected final points percentage, which is what the AHL ranks by.
Win division
Chance of finishing first in the division.
Bye
Chance of finishing high enough to skip the best-of-three first round and go straight to the second round.
Make playoffs
Chance of finishing inside the division's playoff spots.

How much a team's playoff odds moved during last week's games, in percentage points.

How the odds work

Every morning the app replays the rest of the season 5,000 times using the real remaining schedule. Each team's strength comes from its points percentage so far, pulled toward average early in the season when a hot or cold start says less. Results include overtime and shootout losses, which earn a point.

Playoff spots follow the AHL format: every team makes it except the bottom two in the Atlantic, North and Central and the bottom three in the Pacific, ranked by points percentage. This season that's the top five of seven in the Atlantic, top six of eight in the North, top five of seven in the Central, and top seven of ten in the Pacific. Enough top teams skip the best-of-three first round (the bye) that four teams per division reach the second round: three in the Atlantic and Central, two in the North, and one in the Pacific.

Data comes from the AHL's official stats feed and refreshes daily at 3 a.m. Pacific. The weekly arrows compare standings after the last two Sundays, so they roll forward every Monday morning once the weekend's games are final.
